For a long time, a fraudulent email could be spotted by its spelling mistakes. With AI, messages are now well written, personalized, and sometimes backed by voice calls imitating an executive.

## Know the new warning signs

Style is no longer enough. Reliable signals come from context: unusual urgency, a request for payment or new bank details, a link to a login page, an unexpected attachment.

## Set up verification steps

Every transfer request or bank detail change must be confirmed through a second channel, such as a call to the usual number. This simple rule stops most fraud.

## Train regularly

Short sessions with real examples, tailored to each team's work, are more effective than a yearly training. Phishing simulations help measure progress.

## Make reporting easy

An employee who clicked must be able to report it without fear. The faster the alert, the easier it is to lock an account and limit the damage.
